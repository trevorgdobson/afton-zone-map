# Zone: Seven Rila Lakes

The Zone is the cirque of the Seven Rila Lakes (Sedemte rilski ezera, Седемте рилски езера) in the north-west Rila mountains, Bulgaria: seven glacial lakes stepping down a hanging valley from about 2,530 m to 2,100 m, above the spa town of Sapareva Banya. Use it as it really is.

- **Centre:** the middle of the Lower Lake (Dolnoto ezero, Долното езеро), the lowest of the seven, about 42.2109 N, 23.3258 E, the corner where grid squares L, M, 12 and 13 meet. The centre point is in the water.
- **Extent:** roughly 2.5 km around the Lower Lake. That takes in all seven lakes (the farthest, Okoto, is 2.0 km out), both lake huts, the upper lift station and the top two kilometres of the chairlift line; the headwall crest runs just inside the south-west perimeter. Fix the exact perimeter on the first run and never move it.
- **Town:** Sapareva Banya (Сапарева баня), at the northern foot of the range, is where stalkers live, drink and sell. The approach is the mountain road up through the resort village of Panichishte (Паничище, about 6.5 km north-north-west of the centre) to Pionerska hut (х. Пионерска, about 1,530 m) and the lower station of the chairlift, which stands 3.2 km north of the centre, some 200 m beyond the north edge of the grid (above M24). The Institute is in Sofia, to the north.
- **Base map:** there is no government sheet. The field atlas is drawn from data: Copernicus GLO-30 elevation and OpenStreetMap. It has 36 numbered sheets of 4 by 4 squares, sheet 1 at the north-west, reading across, contours in metres (10 m, heavy every 50 m). The atlas is printed in Bulgarian, with place names in Cyrillic as a local map has them; grid letters, numbers and references are Latin. The gazetteer and `terrain.py near` give every name as Latin (Cyrillic) so you can match what the player reads off the sheet.
- **Lie of the land:** a staircase, not a bowl. The headwall is in the south-west: the crest from Otovitsa (Отовица, 2,696 m, outside the perimeter at B7) round through Ezeren Peak (Езерен връх, 2,559 m, H7) stands 2,340 to 2,660 m along the perimeter between bearings 180 and 270, and the highest ground inside is about 2,660 m at E7. From there the lakes step down north-east on rock benches: Salzata 2,532 m (H6), Okoto 2,437 m (F7), Babreka on its own shelf at 2,319 m (G10), Bliznaka 2,244 m (I8), Trilistnika 2,219 m (J10), Ribnoto 2,189 m (L11), and the Lower Lake 2,097 m (L12/L13). The cirque opens to the north and north-east: the Dzherman stream (Джерман) leaves the Lower Lake northward and the perimeter there is down at 1,650 to 1,800 m, the lowest ground in the Zone (1,661 m at S20). The steep ground is the wall under Ezeren Peak and Salzata above Bliznaka (I6, J6: mean slope 45 degrees and more, mapped cliff), the flanks of Haramiyata (Харамията, 2,465 m, K7 to K8) and the east wall above Ribnoto and the Lower Lake (M8 to M10, M12: 30 to 35 degrees), and on the west side the drop into the Skakavitsa valley (E12 to F12, D17 to D21, 30 to 40 degrees). Level ground is scarce and obvious: the lake benches themselves (I9, J10, G10, H11), the flat floor around and north of the Lower Lake (L13, M13, M14, about 2,090 m), and a broad shoulder north-west of the lakes at about 2,300 m (I14, I15); the drag lift from the upper station ends at its north edge (I16). Across the whole circle only 13 per cent of the ground is under 8 degrees; 18 per cent is over 30. The way in is from the north: the chairlift line climbs about 425 m in 1.9 km of grid, from 1,675 m at the grid edge (M24) to the upper station at about 2,095 m (K17-94); Rila Lakes hut (хижа Рилски езера, K16-69) is 150 m south-west of it, and from there mapped trails run about 1 km south over moderate ground (8 to 10 degrees) to the Lower Lake and on up to Seven Lakes hut (хижа Седемте езера, L11-45, about 2,195 m) on the north-east shore of Ribnoto. Check, never assume.
- **Cover:** above about 2,100 m it is open: alpine grass, rock and scree, no trees and almost no shelter. Dwarf pine scrub (клек) is mapped in a band from roughly 1,850 to 2,300 m; off the paths it is slow, tearing ground. Spruce and pine forest covers the lower north and east of the circle below about 2,000 m (rows 14 to 23, columns F to V), including most of the lift line. As mapped, inside the perimeter: grass about half, forest a fifth, dwarf pine a sixth, rock and scree a tenth, water 2 per cent.
- **People and season:** alpine. Snow lies from late autumn well into spring; the steep walls above Bliznaka, Babreka and the east side are avalanche ground in winter (OpenStreetMap marks an avalanche danger zone at L8 and I4, and separate summer and winter routes up to the Razdela saddle, J4). Weather turns fast and there is nowhere to get out of it between the huts. In the real world this is one of the most visited places in the Bulgarian mountains, crowded in summer, served by a single chairlift, two huts and a handful of sheds: 10 buildings are mapped inside the perimeter, no road, no power line. In the fiction all of it has had decades of neglect: the lift does not run, the huts are shells.
- **Units:** metres throughout. The tools answer in metres and feet.
- **Data limits:** the elevation model's true resolution is 30 m, smoothed to the 10 m grid. Lake surfaces and the broad shape are good (six of the seven lakes within 5 m of their published heights); a 5 m rock step, a gully or a boulder field is invisible, summits read up to about 30 m low, and cliffs read as steep slopes. It is a surface model, so in the forest it includes the trees. Babreka reads 2,319 m in this model (and about 2,315 m in the SRTM and ASTER models), not the 2,282 m usually published: trust the model and the atlas contours, which agree with each other.
- **Check:** `python terrain.py --zone rila at L12-99` should report about 2097 m (the surface of the Lower Lake). On Windows run `near` as `python -X utf8 terrain.py --zone rila near ...`: its output carries Cyrillic names.
